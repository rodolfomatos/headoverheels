# D002 — the view and the camera each centred, and the test called one of them

## Verdict: SUPORTADA, with a prediction that was falsifiable and held

The claim, stated before the fix was built:

> Flame's viewfinder anchors at the centre of the viewport, so it adds half a
> window to everything in `world`. `RoomView.render` fits the room to the canvas
> and centres it, and the camera adds half a window on top. If that is the
> fault, pinning `camera.viewfinder.anchor` to `Anchor.topLeft` moves the room's
> top-left corner from (660, 470) to (20, 68).

It moved. A ruler drawn over the live page puts a crosshair at (660, 470); before
the fix that crosshair sat exactly on the room's corner, and after it the room
fills the window with the knight, the ball trap and the chest where they belong.

## The arithmetic that made it a claim and not a guess

`render` computes `offset.dx = (area.width - room.width * scale) / 2`, which for a
non-negative room size is at most `area.width / 2`. With `area.width = 1280` that
ceiling is **640**. The observed corner was at **660**. The page therefore was not
showing the output of `render` at `canvasSize = 1280x800`, whatever the view
reported — and the only thing between the view and the canvas that had not been
read was the camera.

## Why every test missed it

`runtime_render_test.dart` renders with `view.render(canvas)` — the component's
own method, which is the improvement over `renderInto` that made it worth
writing. It is still not the player's path: `FlameGame` draws `world` through its
camera, and a direct call to `view.render` skips that transform entirely. The
test measured the view correctly and the game wrongly, and agreed with the view.

That is the same class as the castle: a check that compares identity and not
content, and a test that builds the thing it tests. Here it is precise enough to
name: **the guard renders one layer below the one the player sees, and every
number it reports is true of that layer only.**

## Relationship to D001

D001's measurement stands and is not superseded: the page's canvas is faithful —
one canvas, intrinsic 1280x800, CSS 1280x800, at the origin, dpr 1. What D001 got
wrong was the *attribution*, and this record corrects it: the distortion is the
game's own camera, composed with the view's own centring. T080's correction
therefore points at the right measurement and the wrong culprit, and both say so.

## What this verdict does not settle

The offscreen test still cannot see the camera. It passes with the fault present
and passed before the fix with the same numbers. Until it composes the camera,
it is a guard over a layer nobody plays on, and the next transform of this kind
will get through it again.
