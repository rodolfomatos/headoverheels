# D008 — the floor treatment is drawn before the objects on it

## What was measured

`switch_1` drew 0 pixels with the room as it stands and 3200 pixels with the room
to itself. The room's children, read at runtime:

```
0  TiledComponent   pos=[-480,0]   size=[1024,512]
2  SwitchEntity     pos=[0,96]     size=[64,32]
5  ConveyorEntity   pos=[-192,96]  size=[1024,32]
```

The floor is child 0 and covers nothing. The conveyor is added after the switch,
is 1024 wide, and is at the same y.

## What was wrong before

The strip bug had been hiding this. `SpriteRegistry` built `Sprite(image)` with no
`srcSize`, so an entity's frame was the whole 384x192 master sheet squeezed into a
64x32 box, spilling ink across neighbouring tiles. The switch looked present
because of the spill. Cutting the sprite to its real cell stopped the spill and
left the switch where it had always been.

**A rendering bug was masking a z-order bug, and fixing the first exposed the
second.**

## The decision

`PuzzleEntity.renderPriority`, default 10. `ConveyorEntity.renderPriority` is 0.
`RoomComponent._spawnEntities` sorts by it, carrying the map index as a tiebreak.

Not "who was declared first". Two kinds of thing share a tile in these rooms: the
floor treatment, and the objects standing on it. A belt spans the room; a switch is
one tile wide. An object standing on the floor is drawn after the floor treatment
whatever order the triggers happen to be in.

The tiebreak is load-bearing rather than tidy: two entities of equal priority must
keep the order the map declared, or a room stops being reproducible and every
render measurement in this project becomes a measurement of the sort.

## What this rests on

`switch_1` at 3200px in the room and 3200px alone — equal, which is what "not
covered" looks like from the outside. `room_render_test.dart` pins the covered set
to empty, so a second covered entity fails the build rather than being noticed by
a player. `aes/graph/render-invariants.yaml` carries the intent and its refutation
as a Z3 core rather than as prose.
